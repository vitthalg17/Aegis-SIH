EVIDENCE, NOT VERDICTS

Every alert can be
argued with.

Mohanty et al. reported 99.35% accuracy on PlantVillage and about 31% on independent field images. So AEGIS never simply names a disease — it hands over the photograph, the confidence and the sensor reading behind the call, and says “needs inspection” when it does not know.

Evidence frame from the revisitThe 4 m photograph the call was made on, not a stock image of the disease.
Class and severity, in plain words“Water stress · Moderate”, never a bare model label.
Confidence, always on screenShown even when it is low — especially when it is low.
The exact coordinateA GPS fix for the zone, not “the north field”.
Why, from the ground nodeThe soil and canopy readings that explain the colour on the map.
What to check nextOne action, in the farmer’s language, on the field display or by SMS.
An honest unknown“Needs inspection” is a first-class outcome, not a failure.

We train on our own imagery

PlantVillage accuracy against independent field images, Mohanty et al. 2016. Lab-trained models collapse outdoors, so ours are trained at our own camera and altitudes.

by field, by date
Splits that refuse to leak

Train and test sets are split by field and by capture date, so the model cannot score well by memorising one afternoon in one plot.

“unknown”
A state we ship on purpose

Below the confidence floor the alert says “needs inspection” and asks for a close-up, rather than inventing a disease name a farmer might spray for.
